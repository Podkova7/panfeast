Exchanging phone numbers and email addresses at professional networking summits, social gatherings, or conferences has historically been a clumsy ritual: typing digits manually into someone else's keypad, dealing with misspelled names, or waiting for a test ring that never arrives. With **NameDrop**, Apple leveraged near-field communication (NFC) and AirDrop to make contact exchange instantaneous, cinematic, and private.

By simply bringing the top of your iPhone within centimeters of another iPhone or Apple Watch, a fluid particle wave of light erupts across both displays. Users can select precisely which phone numbers or email addresses they wish to share, review the recipient's personalized full-screen **Contact Poster**, and save verified credentials with a single tap. In this guide, we break down how NameDrop functions under the hood, how to customize your Contact Poster, and how to manage proximity privacy settings.

## The Mechanical Architecture of NameDrop

NameDrop is not an isolated protocol; it is a secure handshake orchestrating multiple Apple wireless frameworks:

1. **Ultra-Close NFC Proximity Detection:** To prevent accidental handshakes on crowded subways or busy elevators, NameDrop requires high-frequency NFC antennas located at the top edge of the device to be positioned within 3 to 5 centimeters of each other.
2. **Encrypted AirDrop Peer-to-Peer Wi-Fi Handshake:** Once NFC confirms deliberate physical proximity, the operating system spins up a temporary, end-to-end encrypted Wi-Fi Direct tunnel via AirDrop to exchange high-resolution Contact Posters and vCard payloads.
3. **Explicit User Authorization:** A NameDrop session never transmits data silently. Both participants must actively unlock their devices, review on-screen contact fields, and tap "Share" or "Receive Only" before any data is transferred.

## Step-by-Step: Customizing Your Personal Contact Poster

Your Contact Poster is your digital calling card. Setting it up ensures you make a striking professional impression:

### Step 1: Accessing Contact Poster Design
1. Open the **Contacts** (or **Phone**) app on your iPhone.
2. Tap **My Card** at the very top of the contact list.
3. Tap **Contact Photo & Poster**, then tap **Edit**.
4. Tap the **+ (Plus)** button to create a new poster design.

### Step 2: Selecting Visual Styles and Typography
1. Choose your image source: **Camera**, **Photos**, **Memoji**, or a sleek **Monogram**.
2. If selecting a portrait photo, swipe horizontally to apply curated lighting treatments (Studio, Black & White, Color Backdrop).
3. Tap your name at the top to customize typography: select modern bold sans-serif, classic serif, or condensed display fonts, and choose your favorite color tint.
4. Tap **Done** in the top-right corner.
5. Tap **Continue** to approve your circular thumbnail photo used in Messages chat bubbles.

## Executing a NameDrop Session Between Devices

Performing a NameDrop exchange is effortless:

### Step 1: Initiating the Proximity Handshake
1. Ensure both iPhones (running iOS 17 or later) are unlocked.
2. Hold the top edge of your iPhone within a few centimeters of the top edge of the other person's iPhone.
3. Both devices will vibrate with a distinctive dual-pulse haptic tap, and a luminous particle wave will cascade downward from the Dynamic Island across both screens.

### Step 2: Selecting Data and Completing the Exchange
1. Your Contact Poster appears on your screen with two action buttons:
   - **Receive Only:** You receive the other person's contact card without sharing your own information.
   - **Share:** You exchange contact cards bidirectionally.
2. Tap the chevron next to your phone number or email to deselect private numbers if you only wish to share your corporate email address.
3. Tap **Share**: both contact cards populate directly into the respective Contacts databases, complete with posters, profile photos, and job titles.

## Managing Proximity Privacy and Disabling NameDrop

If you prefer to disable proximity gestures entirely:
1. Open **Settings** on your iPhone.
2. Tap **General > AirDrop**.
3. Under the **Start Sharing By** section, toggle **Bringing Devices Together** to **Off**.
4. With this setting disabled, NameDrop gestures are deactivated, while standard AirDrop via the Share Sheet remains fully operational.

For official hardware compatibility tables and Apple Watch Series models supporting NameDrop, visit [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/use-namedrop-iph1b6c664b7/ios).
